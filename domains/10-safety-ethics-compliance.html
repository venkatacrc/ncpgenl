The 10 Verses

Verse 10.01 — Responsible AI principles
Objective 10.1

NVIDIA's Trustworthy AI pillars (aligned with common frameworks): privacy,
safety/security, transparency, non-discrimination/fairness, accountability. Applied
across the whole lifecycle — data (PII removal, 3.09), training (bias-aware),
evaluation (fairness metrics, 10.03), deployment (guardrails), and monitoring (audit,
9.14). Responsible AI is a process, not a one-time check.

Chain → Principles must become deployment practice — how? (10.02)
Verse 10.02 — Responsible AI in the deployment lifecycle
Objective 10.1

Operationalize responsible AI at deploy time: model cards (intended use, limits,
eval results), risk assessment and red-teaming before launch, human oversight
for high-stakes decisions, documented data provenance, and guardrails enforcing
policy at runtime (10.09). Compliance mapping (GDPR/AI Act/sector rules) and an incident
process complete it. Ties transparency (9.14) to enforceable controls.

Chain → A core obligation is fairness — how do we audit for bias? (10.03)
Verse 10.03 — Bias & fairness audit metrics
Objective 10.2

Audit outcomes across protected groups with group fairness metrics:
- Demographic parity: equal positive rate across groups.
- Equal opportunity: equal true-positive rate across groups.
- Equalized odds: equal TPR and FPR across groups.

These can conflict (impossibility results) — choose per context. For generative LLMs,
also probe stereotype/toxicity disparities across demographic prompts. Measure before
and after mitigation.

Chain → Fairness metrics need protected-attribute data — where do biases originate? (10.04)
Verse 10.04 — Sources of bias across the lifecycle
Objective 10.2

Bias enters at many stages: data (historical/representation/sampling bias, skewed
web corpora), labeling (annotator bias), algorithmic (objective amplifies
majority), evaluation (unrepresentative benchmarks), and deployment (feedback
loops, 9.10). Because it compounds, mitigation must span the lifecycle, not just a
post-hoc filter. Identifying the source dictates the fix (data vs training vs
guardrail).

Chain → Monitoring must watch these in production — how? (10.05)
Verse 10.05 — Production safety monitoring
Objective 10.3

Continuously monitor safety signals: toxicity/harmful-content rates, guardrail
trigger rates, jailbreak attempts, PII leakage, refusal/over-refusal rates, and fairness
metrics by segment. Wire into the Domain 9 stack (Prometheus/Grafana) with alerts and
audit logging. Safety monitoring is distinct from performance monitoring and often
required for compliance.

Chain → What tooling classifies content as unsafe at runtime? (10.06)
Verse 10.06 — Content-safety classifiers
Objective 10.3

Dedicated safety classifier models score inputs/outputs for toxicity, harm
categories, and policy violations, feeding guardrail decisions. NVIDIA ships safety NIMs
— e.g. Llama Nemotron Safety / Aegis content-safety models and topic-control /
jailbreak-detection models — invoked as rails. Complements (doesn't replace) alignment:
a classifier catches what the model's training missed.

Stale-knowledge flag: safety-model names (Aegis, Nemotron Safety) and versions
change. VERIFY.

Chain → Detection is half; how do we *reduce* bias, not just observe it? (10.07)
Verse 10.07 — Bias mitigation techniques
Objective 10.4

Mitigate at the stage the bias enters (10.04):
- Pre-processing: rebalance/augment data, remove biased features, curate (3.04).
- In-processing: fairness-constrained training, adversarial debiasing, preference
alignment (DPO) toward fair behavior.
- Post-processing: threshold adjustment per group, output filtering/guardrails.

Always re-measure fairness after mitigation (10.03) and watch for accuracy/fairness
trade-offs. No single technique is sufficient.

Chain → How do we detect bias systematically, not anecdotally? (10.08)
Verse 10.08 — Bias detection & red-teaming
Objective 10.4

Systematic detection: counterfactual/perturbation testing (swap demographic terms,
compare outputs), bias benchmarks (BBQ, StereoSet, ToxiGen, CrowS-Pairs),
disaggregated metrics by segment, and red-teaming (adversarial prompting to elicit
harmful/biased outputs, including automated red-teaming with an attacker LLM). Track
findings over time and gate releases. Detection feeds mitigation (10.07) and guardrails
(10.09).

Chain → Detection and mitigation reduce risk probabilistically — what enforces limits at runtime? (10.09)
Verse 10.09 — NeMo Guardrails & Colang rails
Objective 10.5

NeMo Guardrails enforces policy at runtime via programmable rails, defined in
the Colang language:
- Input rails: block/flag malicious or off-topic prompts (jailbreak/injection, 2.25).
- Dialog rails: constrain conversation to allowed topics/flows.
- Retrieval rails: filter/verify retrieved context (RAG).
- Output rails: block unsafe/PII/hallucinated responses (fact-check/self-check,
5.24).

Rails run independently of the model's cooperation — the enforcement layer that
prompting alone (Domain 2) cannot guarantee.

Stale-knowledge flag: Colang 1.0 vs 2.0 syntax differs. VERIFY.

Chain → How do rails and other layers combine into defense in depth? (10.10)
Verse 10.10 — Defense in depth (guardrail layers)
Objective 10.5

No single control is sufficient; layer them:
1. Data — curation, PII removal (3.09).
2. Model — alignment/SFT/DPO, safety fine-tuning (Domain 5).
3. Prompt — system constraints, grounding, constrained decoding (Domain 2).
4. Rails — NeMo Guardrails input/dialog/retrieval/output + safety classifiers
(10.06, 10.09).
5. Monitoring — safety metrics, audit, red-teaming, incident response (10.05, 10.08,
9.14).

Each layer catches what earlier ones miss. This closes the course: from architecture
(Domain 1) to responsible, monitored, guard-railed production.
